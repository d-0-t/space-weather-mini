# 0007: One pole-to-pole oval map with a continuous glow ramp on a Natural Earth land basemap

The Oval glow drops the two-cap canvases for a single world map (`360x181`, one pixel per OVATION cell, north pole at top), paints the forecast as a continuous color ramp instead of four flat bands, and renders its basemap from the bundled Natural Earth 1:110m land GeoJSON instead of any tile server (first keyed Stadia, then NASA GIBS — both dropped, see options). Grid-edge rows that OVATION fills with a nonzero floor (lat `0`, `-1`, `90`, `-90`) are clipped from paint and counts.

**Status**: accepted (supersedes the tile and presentation parts of ADR-0006; its polling, offline and a11y disciplines stand)

**Considered Options**:

- Keep the two-cap canvases — rejected: the equator row was painted twice, two paint loops and canvas pairs for one dataset, and the 4:1 strips forced Stadia Mercator tiles to be squashed through `object-fit: cover`, misaligning continents against the linear-latitude glow.
- Keep the four flat bands (faint/moderate/strong/intense, frozen Kp palette) — rejected as the paint encoding: a quiet-time max-15 grid reads as three flat greens with no within-band change, and value-1 edge noise painted at full saturation drew phantom patches NOAA's own render never shows.
- GIBS WMS full-world endpoint — rejected: intermittently returns 500s and interprets bbox axis order inconsistently between requests.
- Retain keyed Stadia `alidade_smooth_dark` — rejected: another API key to restrict and deploy, license attribution on-screen, Mercator-vs-equirectangular mismatch, and two canvases' worth of tiles.
- Keep NASA GIBS WMTS tiles (zoom-0 single tile or zoom-1 quadrant wall) with pixel validation, cache-buster retries and a Blue Marble fallback — rejected after the 2026-09-04 incident: the layer's server-side generation proved unstable for hours at a stretch (observed live: the same static 2016 tile URL served a full world, then a right-cut world, then a level-0 pair whose content boundary sat near lon +60-90E instead of the documented 0E, plus constant 500s). Geometry that mutates between requests cannot be pinned client-side - a self-consistent but wrong render passes any seam check - so the tile approach was abandoned entirely.
- Paint land from the bundled Natural Earth 1:110m GeoJSON (`public/ne_110m_land.geojson`, public domain, ~135 KB, precached) — accepted: deterministic geometry through the same `ovalCellPoint` projection as the glow, pixel-aligned by construction, no keys, no attribution, no tile server, offline-safe.

**Consequences**:

- The canvas paints every `Aurora >= 1` cell through `OVAL_RAMP_STOPS` (piecewise-linear five hues: transparent -> green -> yellow -> red -> bright magenta; legend position `pos` and Aurora anchor `value` per stop) via an `ImageData` pass; alpha stops are stored pre-scaled to bytes. Hue boundaries follow intensity: green carries the whole ordinary range (1-15) so quiet maps read like NOAA's own all-green render, yellow starts at 16 (storm onset), red and magenta are reserved for genuine extremes, values past 100 clamp to the magenta end. The legend bar is a CSS gradient derived from the same stops array, so legend and glow cannot drift.
- Band thresholds (`1-5 faint / 6-10 moderate / 11-15 strong / 16+ intense`) remain the semantic layer: the hidden counts table, the view-distance band and color-blind mode (ticket 06) all still key off `auroraBand`.
- The basemap is the bundled Natural Earth 1:110m land GeoJSON painted onto a decorative `aria-hidden` canvas beneath the glow canvas through the same projection, so land and glow cells are pixel-aligned by construction at every viewport (stage `aspect-ratio: 360/181`). Ring longitudes are unwrapped (`projectRing`) and painted at three horizontal offsets, with >180-degree rings closed via the nearer polar edge - a naive `%360` projection made Antarctica's wrapping ring self-intersect the evenodd fill (full-width line at lat ~-17, missing bottom edge). Land fill is `#444444` (3.66:1 on black, user-picked after the first fill measured 1.36:1). The glow frame passes through a 3x3 box blur (`blurGlowFrame`) so the grid's sparse polar speckle and full-width diffuse rows read as soft glow instead of glitch bands. A failed land fetch leaves the ocean black under a still-readable glow. No `gibs` runtime cache remains; `public/*.geojson` joins the PWA precache glob for offline shells.
- Boundary rows are verified feed artifacts (live 2026-09-04: lat `0`/`-1` nonzero with all-zero `1`/`-2`; lat `-90` nonzero next to an all-zero `-89`), invisible on NOAA's own render because its scale fades low values out. Clipping them is a paint/count choice — the parser still accepts the full grid.
- Freshness line restored to the ADR-0006 form (`Forecast Time ... – 30–90 min lead • Updated {age}`).
